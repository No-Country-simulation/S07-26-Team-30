## Overview

Within the IT layer, stranded capacity appears when installed systems are technically available but cannot operate at expected utilization levels. Unlike a complete failure, this represents a gap between what exists and what can effectively be used — resources may remain powered on and operational while failing to deliver full potential value.

## What Operators Observe

Operators may observe patterns such as:

- Compute resources requiring additional configuration before becoming available.
- Hardware capacity that exists but does not match workload requirements.
- Uneven utilization between infrastructure components.

## Why It Happens

Modern IT environments depend on hardware resources, management systems, and operational processes working together. When these elements are misaligned, available resources become difficult to consume efficiently, and part of their potential capacity remains disconnected from active demand.

## Operational Impact

Potential impacts include:

- Reduced utilization of deployed infrastructure.
- Increased complexity in resource management.
- Difficulty measuring available versus usable capacity.

## Example Scenario

Imagine an organization operating a large AI-focused data center. Additional resources have been deployed for future demand, but operational constraints prevent immediate consumption, so a portion of installed capacity remains unused while the organization continues investing.
