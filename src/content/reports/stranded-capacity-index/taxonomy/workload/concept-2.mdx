## Overview

Efficient capacity use depends on continuously coordinating workloads with available capacity. Stranded capacity appears when the relationship between resource availability and workload execution becomes inefficient — resources exist, but utilization does not reflect the environment's computational potential.

## What Operators Observe

Operators may observe patterns including:

- Compute resources remaining unused during periods of potential demand.
- Workloads consuming resources inefficiently relative to their allocation.
- Variations between reported capacity availability and actual workload consumption.

## Why It Happens

Workload execution requires coordination between applications, scheduling systems, infrastructure policies, and operational priorities. When these elements are misaligned, available resources are not consumed efficiently, and capacity exists without translating into productive computation.

## Operational Impact

Potential impacts include:

- Reduced value generated from deployed compute infrastructure.
- Lower efficiency across workload operations.
- Additional coordination between infrastructure and workload teams.

## Example Scenario

Imagine an organization operating an AI environment with significant computational resources. Although the infrastructure is available, workload execution patterns create periods where parts of that capacity remain unused, so the organization may keep expanding while existing resources are not consistently utilized at full potential.
