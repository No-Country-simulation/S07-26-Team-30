## Overview

Within the Facility layer, stranded capacity occurs when physical infrastructure prevents installed computing resources from being fully utilized — even when additional hardware is installed and operational.

## What Operators Observe

Operators may identify this condition through indirect operational signals:

- Installed compute resources remaining partially unused.
- Localized infrastructure constraints while capacity appears available elsewhere.
- Difficulty deploying additional workloads despite unused hardware.

## Why It Happens

Stranded capacity emerges when supporting systems — power delivery, cooling, environmental control — cannot provide the conditions required for additional compute utilization, typically an imbalance rather than a lack of installed hardware. Specific engineering causes are outside the scope of this placeholder report.

## Operational Impact

When physical infrastructure becomes the limiting factor, organizations may experience:

- Reduced usable compute capacity.
- Delayed infrastructure expansion.
- Lower operational efficiency.

## Example Scenario

Consider a hypothetical AI cluster where additional GPU servers are installed and technically available, but a facility limitation prevents bringing them into production — leaving part of the infrastructure idle while demand continues to grow.
