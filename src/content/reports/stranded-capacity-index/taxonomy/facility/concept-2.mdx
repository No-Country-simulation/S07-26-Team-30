## Overview

Not all stranded capacity originates from the same infrastructure constraint. The Facility layer contains several recurring patterns, each a distinct way physical infrastructure limits operational efficiency while producing the same outcome: available infrastructure that cannot translate into productive compute.

## What Operators Observe

Operators may encounter situations such as:

- Resources sufficient in aggregate but unavailable where demand exists.
- Uneven utilization across different areas of the data center.
- Capacity planning driven by physical constraints rather than compute demand.

## Why It Happens

Facility infrastructure comprises interconnected systems that must operate together; as utilization grows, limits in one subsystem reduce the effectiveness of others, leaving installed infrastructure underutilized despite continued demand.

## Operational Impact

This type of constraint may contribute to:

- Lower effective utilization of existing infrastructure.
- Reduced operational flexibility.
- Delays in making installed capacity available to production workloads.

## Example Scenario

Imagine a data center expanded incrementally over time. Although additional compute is installed, differences in the supporting physical environment prevent uniform use of capacity, so planning increasingly focuses on navigating infrastructure limitations rather than allocating compute efficiently.
