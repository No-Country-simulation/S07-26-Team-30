## 2.2. Layer 2: IT & Hardware Infrastructure

Even when sufficient power and cooling are available, limitations within compute platforms, hardware allocation strategies, or infrastructure management processes can prevent effective utilization of installed capacity — compute resources that are technically available but cannot be efficiently assigned to productive workloads.

### 2.2.1. Unbalanced Thermal Throttling (UTT)

Frequency reduction in specific nodes caused by regional thermal misalignment, degrading whole-cluster throughput.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- GPU clocks stepping down on individual racks while adjacent racks run at full boost.
- Temperature deltas across the cluster exceeding 10 °C under identical workloads.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Reduced training throughput: slow nodes hold back synchronized steps across the entire job.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Cooling distribution is imbalanced while scheduling is thermal-agnostic, so hot nodes throttle instead of the workload moving or the cooling adapting.

### 2.2.2. Underutilized Interconnect Bandwidth (UIB)

Network latency and inefficiency that keep high-end GPUs idle waiting for data.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- Fabric utilization well below capacity while GPU compute utilization drops during all-reduce and checkpoint phases.
- Long tail latencies on specific paths despite low average utilization.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Expensive GPU-seconds burned on data movement instead of computation across every training step.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Topology-unaware placement or static routing spreads traffic unevenly, and orchestration does not co-schedule data movement with compute.

### 2.2.3. Power Cap Buffering (PCB)

Rigid per-server power caps that prevent optimal performance even when the facility has headroom available.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- Servers pinned below their allowed power envelope while PDUs report surplus capacity.
- Sustained power capping events on nodes that never trip facility limits.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Predictable performance loss bounded only by the cap, not by actual thermal or power constraints.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Power caps are assigned statically at provisioning time and never revisited against real telemetry, so safety buffers become permanent performance ceilings.
