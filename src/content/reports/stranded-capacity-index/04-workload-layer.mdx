### 2.3. Layer 3: Workload & Orchestration

The Workload layer addresses orchestration, scheduling, and workload placement. Even with power, cooling, and hardware fully available, scheduling decisions — or their absence — can leave the most expensive compute hardware in the facility idle, waiting on synchronization, thermal awareness, or overly conservative reservations.

#### 2.3.1. Non-Aware Thermal Scheduling (NATS)

Planning of massive AI jobs without considering the thermal or energy state of the facility.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- Jobs packed into thermally constrained zones while chilled zones sit empty.
- Hot spots forming at a predictable cadence that mirrors the job schedule.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Throttling cascades that reduce job throughput despite nominal capacity being available elsewhere.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Schedulers select nodes from a free-list, ignoring thermal and power telemetry, so placement actively creates the constraints the facility then has to manage reactively.

#### 2.3.2. Barrier Synchronization Idle (BSI)

Dead time for high-performance GPUs while waiting for synchronization with slower nodes.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- GPU utilization dropping in step with all-reduce and barrier phases of distributed training.
- Total step time significantly exceeding the compute phase of the slowest node.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Expensive GPU-seconds spent idle on every training step, multiplied across thousands of steps.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Distributed training is only as fast as its slowest member; without topology-aware scheduling and co-design, stragglers force the entire cluster to wait.

#### 2.3.3. Over-Provisioned Job Allocation (OPJA)

Excessive resource reservation by developers to avoid out-of-memory (OOM) failures.

<Label>What You See</Label>

- Jobs requesting far more memory and accelerators than their measured steady-state usage.
- Queue lengths growing while aggregate cluster utilization stays moderate.

<Label>What It Costs</Label>

- Reserved-but-unused resources that cannot be granted to other jobs — stranded capacity created by the scheduler's own allocation policy.

<Label>Why It Occurs</Label>

Developers size requests for worst-case peaks as insurance against OOM, and the scheduler has no incentive or mechanism to reclaim idle reservations.
