## 3. PSCI Measurement Methodology & Benchmarks

### 3.1. Key Metrics & Formulas

The PSCI formalizes stranded capacity as a measurable ratio between what is provisioned and what becomes productive compute.

**PSCI (%) = (Provisioned capacity − Productive compute) / Provisioned capacity × 100**

Each operational layer contributes a sub-index, so losses can be attributed and prioritized:

- **PSCI-facility**: thermal and power capacity contracted but not delivered to IT loads.
- **PSCI-it**: hardware capacity installed but not converted into FLOPs.
- **PSCI-workload**: schedulable capacity lost to orchestration inefficiency.

**MFU vs. PUE alignment.** PUE measures facility energy efficiency but is blind to compute output; MFU measures what fraction of theoretical FLOPs a workload achieves but is blind to energy. PSCI relates both: a facility with excellent PUE and a workload with low MFU still strands the majority of its contracted compute. Operators should track PUE and MFU together, with PSCI as the single reconciliation metric.

### 3.2. Baseline Hyperscale Benchmarks

Across surveyed hyperscale environments, typical stranded capacity ranges from **22% to 38%** of contracted capacity, depending on design maturity and operational tooling. The dominant loss layer varies by facility generation: older facilities lose more at the Facility layer, while newer, thermally efficient facilities shift the balance toward Workload losses.

| Layer | Typical loss range | Dominant pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facility | 8–15% | Static Thermal Headroom, Power Distribution Stranding |
| IT | 6–12% | Unbalanced Thermal Throttling, Power Cap Buffering |
| Workload | 6–14% | Barrier Synchronization Idle, Over-Provisioned Allocation |
